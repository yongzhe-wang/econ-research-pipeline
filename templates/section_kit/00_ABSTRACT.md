# 00 — Abstract

Target length: **180–230 words** (AER / TAR caps at ~250).

## The six-slot abstract

Every top-journal accounting/public-econ abstract fills the same six slots, almost always in this order:

1. **Hook / motivation** (1 sentence) — why the question matters in 25 words.
2. **What we do** (1–2 sentences) — design + sample + period in plain English.
3. **Identification handle** (1 sentence) — what variation, what shock, what comparison group.
4. **Headline result** (1–2 sentences) — sign, magnitude in % or SD, statistical significance implicit (don't say "*p*<0.01" in an abstract).
5. **Mechanism / heterogeneity** (1 sentence, optional) — the *why*.
6. **Contribution** (1 sentence) — what literature this updates.

## Three exemplar abstracts, annotated

Quoted ≤30 words each (copyright). Full text available at the publisher links.

### Hoopes, Mescall & Pittman 2012 *TAR* — IRS audits and tax avoidance

> "We extend research on the determinants of corporate tax avoidance to include the role of Internal Revenue Service (IRS) monitoring."

**Slot 1+2 (hook + what we do).** Notice: no throat-clearing, no "tax avoidance is an important topic." Verb in sentence one is `extend`. The contribution claim is structural ("include the role of...").

### Chen, Chen, Cheng & Shevlin 2010 *JFE* — Family firms and tax aggressiveness

> "Using multiple measures to capture tax aggressiveness and founding family presence, we find that family firms are less tax aggressive than their non-family counterparts."

**Slots 2+4 (what we do + headline).** Sign and direction stated bluntly. "Less ... than ..." is a comparison, which makes the result interpretable.

### Cengiz, Dube, Lindner & Zipperer 2019 *QJE* — Minimum-wage effects

> "We estimate the effect of minimum wages on low-wage jobs using 138 prominent state-level minimum-wage changes between 1979 and 2016 in the United States."

**Slots 2+3 (what we do + identification handle).** Note the exact-number disclosure ("138 changes") — this is the QJE house style. Readers can replicate the count.

## What NOT to write in an abstract

- "This paper is the first to..." (referees groan).
- "We use a difference-in-differences design" with no description of the shock.
- A literature-review summary masquerading as motivation ("Prior literature has examined X. Y. Z. In this paper..."). Cut to the question.
- p-values, t-stats, R².
- The word "comprehensive."

## Skeleton for our paper

### Design B — Golden Tax Phase IV (2022) enforcement shock

```text
[HOOK] Whether stronger tax-administration technology deters corporate
tax avoidance is a first-order question for emerging-market tax design.

[WHAT WE DO] We use the 2022 nationwide rollout of China's Golden Tax
Phase IV (GTP-IV) — a real-time, big-data invoicing-and-monitoring
platform — as a quasi-natural experiment, exploiting the staggered
exposure of A-share firms across <<industries / regions / VAT-invoice-
intensity quartiles>> over <<2018Q1–2024Q4>>.

[ID HANDLE] Treatment intensity is identified from <<pre-period
VAT-invoice intensity × post-2022 indicator>>; identification rests on
parallel pre-trends and the assumption that GTP-IV deployment timing is
uncorrelated with unobserved avoidance shocks.

[HEADLINE] Firms in the top quartile of treatment exposure experience a
<<X.X percentage-point>> increase in cash effective tax rates relative
to the bottom quartile (significant at the <<1>>% level, robust to
Callaway–Sant'Anna and Sun–Abraham estimators), corresponding to
<<Y>>% of the sample-mean cash ETR.

[MECHANISM] The effect concentrates in <<state-owned / VAT-input-
intensive / low-quality-auditor / opaque>> firms and is absent in
<<benchmark group>>, consistent with <<information-asymmetry reduction
/ deterrence>> as the operating channel.

[CONTRIBUTION] The findings provide the first event-study evidence on
real-time tax-administration digitization in the world's largest
emerging tax economy, complementing US monitoring evidence in Hoopes,
Mescall, and Pittman (2012) and Hanlon, Hoopes, and Shroff (2014).
```

### Design A — Within-firm signing-auditor gender-pair switch

```text
[HOOK] Whether the demographic composition of the engagement-partner
pair affects clients' tax-planning aggressiveness is unresolved.

[WHAT WE DO] We use <<NNN>> within-firm switches in the signing-auditor
gender pair for A-share listed firms over <<2010–2023>>, comparing
firm-years before and after the switch within the same firm and
auditor-office.

[ID HANDLE] Identification exploits the timing of signing-auditor
rotations that are plausibly orthogonal to client tax preferences,
controlling for firm, auditor-office, and year fixed effects.

[HEADLINE] Switches from a single-gender to a mixed-gender pair are
associated with a <<X.X percentage-point>> decrease in book–tax
differences (<<Y>> SD of the sample), with effects concentrated in
firms with weaker internal governance.

[MECHANISM] The effect operates through <<higher engagement hours /
greater issuance of qualified opinions / increased tax-fee
disclosure>>, consistent with cognitive-diversity gains documented in
He, Li, Monroe, and Si (2021) and Khavis, Sheneman, and Szerwo (2025).

[CONTRIBUTION] The paper extends signing-auditor diversity research to
the tax-planning setting and complements DeFond, Qi, Si, and Zhang
(2025) by isolating composition effects from expertise effects.
```

## Drafting checklist

- [ ] Word count between 180 and 230.
- [ ] First sentence does not start with "Tax avoidance is..."
- [ ] Exactly one number in the abstract (the headline magnitude).
- [ ] Identification handle is one self-contained sentence.
- [ ] Mechanism is one sentence, not three.
- [ ] No citations in the abstract unless absolutely necessary (one is acceptable for the directly competing paper — e.g., DeFond et al. 2025).
- [ ] No "comprehensive," "novel," "important," "first to."
